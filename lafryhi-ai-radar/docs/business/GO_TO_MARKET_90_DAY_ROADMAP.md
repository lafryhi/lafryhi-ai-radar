# Go-To-Market 90-Day Roadmap

**Version:** 1.0  
**Start date:** 2026-07-29

## Purpose

This roadmap is a learning plan, not a feature roadmap. Its goal is to progress from early observation to evidence of repeat value and genuine willingness to pay while changing as little as necessary at each stage.

## Phase 1 — First 20 Users

**Duration:** Weeks 1–2

### Objectives

- Reach 20 real users.
- Observe onboarding behavior.
- Validate understanding of Decision Briefs.
- Collect qualitative feedback.
- Identify the first high-response segment.

### Acquisition Channels

- LinkedIn.
- Facebook.
- YouTube.
- Google Cloud communities.
- Gemini communities.
- Founder network.

### Decision Gate

Proceed when there is direct evidence showing where users enter, where they hesitate, whether they reach a first Decision Brief, and which initial segment responds most strongly. If activation is weak, improve the primary onboarding bottleneck before increasing acquisition.

## Phase 2 — Validate Value

**Duration:** Weeks 3–4

The primary research question is:

> Did the Decision Brief help you make a better business decision?

Do not substitute a general satisfaction question such as “Did you like the product?” Approval is not proof of value. Ask for the decision, action, avoided work, improved confidence, or changed outcome.

### Decision Gate

Evidence should include concrete examples of useful business decisions, plus clear reasons why Briefs were not useful. If users understand the product but cannot demonstrate value, revisit positioning, segment fit, and the Brief experience before expanding scope.

## Phase 3 — First 100 Users

**Duration:** Month 2

### Objectives

- Reach 100 real users.
- Establish a clear activation funnel.
- Observe an initial retention signal.
- See repeated Decision Brief usage.
- Identify the strongest customer segment.

### Decision Gate

Scale the best-performing channel and segment only when acquisition leads to activation and some users return for another Brief. Raw registrations without useful Briefs or repeat usage do not satisfy this gate.

## Phase 4 — First Paying Customers

**Duration:** Month 3

### Objectives

- Win the first genuine paid customer.
- Reach 10 paying customers as a stretch goal.
- Record reasons for upgrading.
- Record objections and cancellation reasons.
- Validate willingness to pay **$9/month**.

Paddle Sandbox activity does not count as payment evidence. Revenue evidence begins only when Paddle Live is intentionally enabled under a separate, approved operational decision and a customer independently completes a real charge.

### Decision Gate

Confirm whether payment is driven by repeated Decision Brief value, usage capacity, trust, or another reason. Do not infer pricing validation from checkout intent alone.

## Phase 5 — Repeatable Learning Loop

Run this loop every week:

> Acquire  
> → Activate  
> → Observe  
> → Interview  
> → Analyze  
> → Improve one bottleneck  
> → Measure again

The improvement step is intentionally singular. Concentrated changes make it easier to understand what influenced the result.

## Weekly Execution Schedule

| Week | Primary objective | Target metric | Main channel | Customer-learning activity | Decision gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Recruit an initial observation cohort | 10 real users | Founder network, LinkedIn, and Facebook | Observe onboarding; interview at least 3 users | Can users explain the product and begin without live help? |
| 2 | Complete the first-user cohort | 20 cumulative users | LinkedIn, Facebook, and direct outreach | Review first-value paths and interview non-activators | Is there a dominant onboarding bottleneck and responsive segment? |
| 3 | Test Decision Brief usefulness | At least 10 generated Briefs with follow-up | LinkedIn and Facebook | Ask about the actual decision before and after the Brief | Can users name a decision improved by the Brief? |
| 4 | Validate trust and repeat intent | Useful feedback rate trending toward 60% | Facebook, LinkedIn, and Gemini communities | Interview useful and not-useful respondents | Is value strong enough to expand acquisition? |
| 5 | Establish a measurable funnel | Funnel stages measurable for the cohort | LinkedIn and Facebook | Compare activated and non-activated users | Which single stage loses the most qualified users? |
| 6 | Improve the largest bottleneck | Measurable improvement at one stage | Best channel from Weeks 1–5 | Usability follow-up on the changed stage | Did the targeted metric improve without harming trust? |
| 7 | Test segment concentration | 60 cumulative users | Best-performing community | Compare activation and value by segment | Which segment demonstrates the strongest useful behavior? |
| 8 | Reach 100 real users | 100 cumulative users | Winning channel plus referrals | Interview returning users | Is there repeat Brief generation and an initial retention signal? |
| 9 | Clarify upgrade value | Qualified Pro interest recorded | Direct conversations | Ask what recurring value would justify $9/month | Are upgrade reasons tied to demonstrated product value? |
| 10 | Prepare real-payment readiness decision | Documented billing readiness and objections | Activated-user outreach | Conduct willingness-to-pay interviews | Is there enough evidence to consider Paddle Live separately? |
| 11 | Seek the first genuine paid customer | 1 genuine paid customer, if Live is approved | Highest-value cohort | Record upgrade trigger and buying context | Did an independent customer pay because of real value? |
| 12 | Improve paid conversion learning | Conversion evidence; 10 paid as stretch | Referrals and winning channel | Interview buyers, objections, and cancellations | Are upgrade drivers repeatable rather than exceptional? |
| 13 | Complete 90-day review | Evidence-backed next-quarter plan | All validated channels | Synthesize behavior, interviews, retention, and revenue | Continue, focus, reposition, or stop initiatives based on evidence |

## Operating Constraints

- Do not optimize acquisition volume before understanding activation.
- Do not equate positive comments with useful outcomes.
- Do not treat sandbox transactions as revenue.
- Do not build a major feature to satisfy one interview.
- Record material strategic changes in the [Decision Log](./DECISION_LOG.md).
